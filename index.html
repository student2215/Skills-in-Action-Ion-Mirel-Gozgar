01 — About this portfolio

Turning module activities into employability skills

Throughout the module I worked through the full process of developing and presenting a new venture: generating an idea, researching the market, testing the numbers, and pitching the concept to an audience. The focus of this portfolio is not the venture itself. It is the set of transferable skills that the process helped me build.

I first identified the employability skills I needed to develop. I then took part in the module tasks that would let me practise them, and I kept evidence of my progress such as notes, drafts, slides and photographs. The sections below bring that evidence together and reflect on what I learned.

The skills I focus on are drawn from widely used graduate-employability frameworks and map directly onto what employers look for in business and enterprise graduates.

02 — Skills I developed

Six employability skills, mapped to the module

For each skill I explain what it means, how I developed it during the new venture work, and what it shows an employer.

01

Research and Market Analysis

The ability to gather, organise and interpret information so that a decision can be supported by evidence.

How I built it: I researched the market for the venture concept, read industry and consumer sources, and used tools such as PESTEL and competitor comparison to make sense of what I found. I learned to separate reliable sources from weak ones and to summarise findings clearly.

What it shows: I can turn scattered information into a clear picture that guides a business decision.

02

Commercial and Financial Awareness

Understanding how an organisation makes money and what makes an idea viable in practice.

How I built it: Working on the venture numbers meant building revenue estimates, start-up costs and a simple break-even view. I had to question my own assumptions and check whether the figures were realistic rather than optimistic.

What it shows: I can think about cost, value and risk, not just the idea on its own.

03

Communication and Presentation

Expressing ideas clearly in writing and in person so that an audience understands and stays engaged.

How I built it: Presenting the venture concept pushed me to structure a message for a specific audience, design clear slides, and answer questions on the spot. I practised pacing, eye contact and keeping each point short.

What it shows: I can present a proposal with confidence and adapt it to the people in the room.

04

Teamwork and Collaboration

Working with others towards a shared goal, sharing the load fairly and handling disagreement well.

How I built it: The module involved discussion, peer feedback and shared tasks. I learned to listen before responding, to build on other people's points, and to agree who was doing what so that deadlines were met.

What it shows: I contribute to a team and help keep it moving rather than working in isolation.

05

Problem-Solving and Critical Thinking

Breaking a problem down, weighing the options, and choosing a course of action for sound reasons.

How I built it: Developing a venture is a series of problems, from an unclear market to a budget that does not balance. I learned to pause, set out the options, and justify the choice I made instead of guessing.

What it shows: I approach difficulties methodically and can explain the thinking behind my decisions.

06

Digital and Time-Management Skills

Using digital tools effectively and organising work so that deadlines are met without a rush at the end.

How I built it: I used presentation, document and spreadsheet software, and I built this webpage itself. Balancing the module tasks with other commitments taught me to plan backwards from the deadline and track my own progress.

What it shows: I am comfortable with digital tools and I manage my workload independently.

04 — Reflection

What I learned about my own development

Looking back over the module, the skill that developed most for me was communication. At the start I found presenting uncomfortable, and I relied too much on reading from my notes. By preparing properly and focusing on one clear message per slide, I became more confident and the audience stayed with me.

My research also improved. Early on I accepted sources too quickly. I learned to check where information came from and to compare more than one view before drawing a conclusion. This changed how I approached every other task, because a weak starting point weakened everything built on it.

If I repeated the module, I would plan my time earlier and keep a tidier record of my evidence as I went, rather than gathering it near the end. The main lesson is that these skills are built through practice and honest review, and I will carry that approach into placements and future work.
